Recently went through my journal, looking at this month in years past. Seems November has been a time of increased shop time for me each year, fewer things to do outside and more opportunity for woodworking.

Yesterday was no exception, the entire morning passed happily in the blink of an eye as I fitted final interior details on my [Dutch tool chest](https://lostartpress.com/products/dutch-tool-chests) (which I glued-up last November, and have been contentedly using for much of this year).

Thankfully, I’m not making gifts this year, so the pressure is off, and the mood is largely “let’s see where we get”. A good mood to seek.

***

- Mandy Brown”s “[Against disliteracy](https://aworkinglibrary.com/writing/against-disliteracy)” presents reading as essential to liberty, a bulwark against destructive forces and a foundation for a better future. It’s a tour de force of favourite authors and books: my two favourite Ursulas (Le Guin and Franklin), Alberto Manguel, W.E.B. Du Bois, and more. Here’s a taste: “Let the machines read for us while we lay about, listening to neat summaries of books and papers, smug in our time-saving abilities—though what, precisely, we have saved that time for remains to be seen. Let the machines write for us too, displacing thinking and making with recording, every answer to every possible question available at the barest command. No curiosity, no desire that cannot be immediately sated and therefore squashed before it comes to life.”
- Jack Cheng’s “[Studio Project #3: A House for Oneself](https://jackcheng.com/studio-project-3-a-house-for-oneself/)” illustrates how reading is about more than just words. Cheng walks us through his architectural and site design process for a property built on the principles of [Christopher Alexander](https://christopher-alexander-ces-archive.org/biography/). It’s a wonderful account of carefully reading a _site_, considering it in conversation with its context, and iteratively designing a house on it. (This was part of [Cheng’s experience in the “Building Beauty”](https://jackcheng.com/sunday/379-back-to-school/) program.)
- Austin Kleon’s “[Michael Crichton, psychology, and AI](https://austinkleon.com/2026/11/10/michael-crichton-psychology-and-ai/)” does indeed cover those three subjects, nicely linking the three.
- Ava writes about “[chronic illness and personal time](https://blog.avas.space/illness-me-time/)”. A good reminder, for those who don’t live with chronic illness.
- [Jennifer Robson’s analysis of the Stellantis situation](https://robsonj.substack.com/p/something-quick-on-stellantisrochel) left me pondering the enduring, “sticky” value of investing in people over companies.
- Two 99pi episodes: “[100 Objects #19: Western Electric 500 Series](https://99percentinvisible.org/episode/100-objects-019-western-electric-500-series/)”, which has me thinking about latency in audio and visual calls (and building for repairability, when incentives are right!); “[(Not Just) Another Brick in the Wall](https://99percentinvisible.org/episode/685-not-just-another-brick-in-the-wall/)”, which taught me that brick often reflected _where it was made_ in its appearance, with unique combinations of local stone producing radically different bricks (also helped me understand why brick fell out of structural use, something I’ve wondered at since reading Stewart Brand praise it in _How Buildings Learn_).
- Love the idea of going on [a trip with a focus on craft](https://foolishwood.substack.com/p/slovenia-trip).

All the best for the week ahead!

Lucas
