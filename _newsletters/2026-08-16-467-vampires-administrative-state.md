So, I don’t think vampires exist. But if they did, in our world, imagine their perspective on technological and political trends.

As governments digitalize, with increasing embrace of digital identifiers and records, vampires’ great asset—longevity—becomes an even strong liability: your ageless nature is that much harder to hide if you need to interact with the state, and the state’s way of making you legible is to digitize your existence.

But vampires have been around a while. They’ve witnessed the rise of the administrative state. Probably met Max Weber _and_ James Scott. So they know a thing or two about how states work—and how to influence them. 

If you were a vampire, coolly assessing the rise in digitalized government identification and record keeping, how might you respond? You couldn’t well agitate on the front lines against such changes—your unchanging nature isn’t well-suited for high-profile appearances. But you could, with the considerable wealth you could acquire over a long life (carefully hidden though it must be), influence and invest in _others_ doing the agitating for you.

I’m not saying that vampires are behind the anti-digital-ID movement. But I’m also not saying they’re _uninvolved_. At least, I wouldn’t be saying those things if I think they existed. Which, again, I don’t! (If vampires aren’t your thing, consider that [Adaline](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Age_of_Adaline) would have the same problem—though probably easier to manage as an individual than as a secret society.)
